Example of two universally equivalent fields which are not elementarily equivalent

1 September 2026

Sometimes it is useful to ask ourselves whether we do something meaningful (or, as I normally ask myself in Russian, “А не фигню ли я делаю?”). My PhD thesis is dedicated to the universal equivalence of linear groups over some kinds of rings (fields and local (associative unital) rings with $1/2$). The elementary equivalence of these structures was studied sixty years ago; the classical result belongs to A. I. Mal’cev: linear groups $\mathrm{G}_n(K)$ and $\mathrm{G}_m(L)$, where $\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{GL}, \mathrm{SL}, \mathrm{PGL}, \mathrm{PSL}$, $n, m \geqslant 3$, $K$ and $L$ are fields of characteristic $0$, are elementarily equivalent iff $m=n$ and fields $K$ and $L$ are elementarily equivalent\footnote{A. I. Mal’cev, “On elementary properties of linear groups,” Probl. Mat. Mech., 110–132 (1961).}. So the natural question is: does universal equivalence of these particular structures — linear groups — contribute anything new to the question under consideration, or is it just the same as elementary equivalence? In other words, keeping in mind Mal’cev’s theorem, {\bf are there any universally equivalent but not elementarily equivalent fields}?

And the answer is yes: $\overline{\mathbb Q}\ueq \overline{\mathbb Q}(t),$ but $\overline{\mathbb Q}\not\eeq \overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$.

Firstly, show that these fields are not elementarily equivalent. Indeed, $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is algebraically closed, whereas $\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$ is not. For example, we cannot extract a square root of $t$, and this can be expressed as a first-order formula:

\[\forall x\exists y (y^2 = x).\]

(If we could, then consider a square root $\frac{p(t)}{q(t)}$ of $t$: $\left(\frac{p(t)}{q(t)}\right)^2 = t$. Then $p(t)^2 = tq(t)^2$, and $2\deg p(t) = 1 + 2\deg q(t)$, which is impossible.)

To show that these fields are universally equivalent, consider the following extension tower:

\[\overline{\mathbb Q}\subset\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\subset\overline{\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)}.\]

Here $\overline{\mathbb Q}\eeq \overline{\,\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\,}$ as algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$ (a general fact). And the existential theories are embedded like this:

\[\Th_{\exists}\!\left(\overline{\mathbb Q}\right) \subseteq \Th_{\exists}\!\left(\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\right) \subseteq \Th_{\exists}\!\left(\overline{\,\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\,}\right),\]

where $\Th_{\exists}\left(\overline{\mathbb Q}\right) = \Th_{\exists}\left(\overline{\,\overline{\mathbb Q}(t)\,}\right)$, because their full first-order theories are the same. Hence, all existential theories coincide, and $\overline{\mathbb Q}\exeq \overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$, which is equivalent to $\overline{\mathbb Q}\ueq \overline{\mathbb Q}(t)$.
